AI Research & Paper Mentoring for Ambitious Students

Paper Mentor AI pairs motivated high-school students with experienced AI researchers and graduate mentors to design, build, and publish high-level AI research. Our students frequently publish in competitive journals— including Stanford Intersect, Journal of Emerging Investigators (JEI), National High School Journal of Science (NHSJS), International Journal of High School Research (IJHSR), STEM Fellowship Journal, American Journal of Student Research (AJSR), and the Journal of High School Science (JHSS)—giving their university applications a powerful edge. We also provide strong recommendation letters to support college applications or internship placements for outstanding students.

All research is guided 1:1 by mentors from top universities such as Cornell, Columbia, and Stanford, with deep experience in AI, computer science, and data-driven research.

Where Our Mentors Come From

Our mentors are graduate students, researchers, and practitioners with strong AI and STEM backgrounds, trained at some of the world’s most selective universities.

Many mentors are current or former graduate students and researchers at Cornell University and Stanford University, with additional experience from other top global institutions.

Why Students Work with Paper Mentor AI

We focus on one outcome: helping students create real, technically rigorous AI research and polished papers that genuinely matter for their growth and applications.

  • 1:1 mentorship tailored to each student’s background, schedule, and long-term goals.
  • Mentors are AI-focused graduate students, researchers, and industry practitioners from top universities.
  • Project-based learning: students implement real models and experiments, not just follow pre-built tutorials.
  • Structured support for research framing, experiments, analysis, and full paper writing.
  • Guidance on targeting appropriate student journals and conferences, including timeline and submission strategy.
  • For privacy reasons, full past student papers are shared only on request with families, during calls or by private link.

What Working with Paper Mentor AI Looks Like

Students work in small, focused blocks each week—meeting with mentors, running experiments, and refining their paper, just like in a real research lab.

A typical week combines mentor meetings, independent coding and reading, and preparation of figures, results, and drafts. Students learn how to:

  • Read, understand, and replicate ideas from modern AI research papers.
  • Build and train models in Python (often with PyTorch, TensorFlow, or scikit-learn).
  • Design experiments, evaluate models, and interpret results.
  • Write a clear, polished research paper suitable for journals and competitions.

Families can review anonymized excerpts or full sample papers during a consultation call, upon request.
